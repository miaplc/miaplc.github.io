within everyone's reach.

MiaPLC is an ESP32-based PLC programmed in ladder from an editor in your browser, with networks, functions, data blocks, Online view and diagnostics, just like on the factory floor. For a few euros you get a real board on your bench and start learning industrial automation.

Why MiaPLC

When I was at school, a PLC was a mirage.

It cost too much to put one in every student's hands: labs had a few, always the same ones, and we looked at them more than we used them. If you wanted to practise at home, you simply couldn't.

Today a microcontroller costing a few euros has power that was unthinkable back then, and modern tools, artificial intelligence included, let a single person build something that truly gets close to the industrial world.

I spent my time building this version of a PLC: very similar to the ones you find in real plants, but made on cheap, open hardware.

“MiaPLC is not only about cutting costs for schools. It's about starting a path: the concepts you learn here are the same ones you'll find in the development environments of the big automation brands. And in the meantime you can use it for a serious hobby project.”
— Denys, PLC & Automation Engineer
  • One board per student, not one per lab
  • Programmed from the browser: no licences, nothing to install on school PCs
  • The same ideas as industry: scan cycle, networks, FCs, DBs, Online
  • Free software (GPL-3.0): you can study how it works inside, too
What it does

A complete PLC inside an ESP32

The firmware runs your program in a bytecode virtual machine, scan after scan. The editor is served by the PLC itself: open it in the browser and start programming.

Ladder like in the factory

Networks with title and comment, contacts, coils, SET/RESET, edges, comparisons, math and conversion boxes.

http://192.168.4.1

Editor in the browser

Nothing to install: the editor runs on the PLC. Compile, Download, Run, the way you're used to.

Online and diagnostics

Watch power flow through contacts, timers counting, DB values changing. Event log, scan times, RAM.

Structured blocks

Main, StartUp, functions (FC) with an interface, retentive DBs, structures, arrays. FCs in ST and C too.

Timers, counters, numbers

TON, TOF, TP, CTU, CTD, CTUD; types from BOOL to REAL and TIME; linear scaling for analog values.

Communication

Ethernet and WiFi, Modbus TCP, µLink remote I/O modules, touch HMI panel.

The editor

Program in ladder, watch it work live

Motor start/stop with self-holding, in Online view: closed contacts and energised coils turn green.

  • Symbol table and cross-references
  • System bits: first scan, 1/2/10 Hz clocks
  • Multilingual interface
  • Firmware update from the editor or over USB
  • RAM and scan time analysis
  • PLC backup and restore

The family

Four pieces working together

MiaPlc

Firmware and editor

The PLC virtual machine, the scan cycle, the modules (network, clock, I/O) and the web editor.

MiaConfig

Configurator

For custom electronic boards and advanced projects: draw your board (inputs, outputs, Ethernet, clock) and it builds a tailored firmware. Not needed with a ready board. Windows, Linux, macOS.

µLink

Remote I/O

A wired-Ethernet bus for I/O modules: other ESP32 boards or even an Arduino Uno with a W5100/W5500 shield.

MiaHmi

Operator panel

A 10.1" touch panel on ESP32-P4 that reads and writes PLC data.

Next steps

Where MiaPLC is heading

The project moves forward one step at a time. Tests are done within the limits of what I can buy and design, so the timing is not a promise: this is the road, and the last points are dreams for now.

  1. in progress

    The first official board

    The first PLC designed specifically for MiaPLC, using Espressif modules already certified for the radio part.

  2. partly done

    Testing every chip in the configurator

    MiaConfig already lists many chips and boards. Some are tested because they're on my bench; the others have to be bought and tested one by one.

  3. planned

    Basic µLink modules

    I/O modules based on the P4 and KSZ multiport switches, with protection against downstream wiring mistakes and connectors designed to plug easily into boards designed by users themselves.

  4. planned

    Physical tests with 50, 100 and 500 boards

    The simulator and tests with a few modules say a lot, but the bus has to be tested for real, at ever larger numbers.

  5. being evaluated

    Beyond the P4: more powerful chips

    I'm evaluating the Allwinner T113 and T156 processors. For powerful chips the question mark is always the same: cost and bringing up the DDR memory.

  6. planned

    Cybersecurity and EU rules

    Before certification, the firmware has to meet the cybersecurity rules: requirements of the RED directive (EN 18031 standard), the Cyber Resilience Act, mandatory passwords never left at factory defaults, protected critical commands (RUN/STOP, Download, forcing), signed updates and a process to report and fix vulnerabilities.

  7. the dream

    Boards certified for industry

    Getting the boards certified and ready for integration in industrial plants, on the more powerful platforms (T156, Zynq and the like).

Complete example

Not two lamps: a real plant

Belacqua is a farm of 100 beluga sturgeons in 20 tanks, run by a MiaPLC PLC with two µLink I/O modules and a touch panel: oxygen and meals tank by tank, recirculation with energy saving, 146 alarms, 369 ladder networks. All downloadable, with the manual.
